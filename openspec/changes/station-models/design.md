# Design

## Context

Stations are 4 px rings on the track. Close up the buildings hide them; far away they look like the
line. The owner asked for models close and beacons far, with a crossfade.

Everything about glTF in deck.gl was learned during the train models (#16) and is reused rather than
rediscovered.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals**

- Stations findable at a glance, in the city centre and across the whole network.
- Models that distinguish the four modes.
- One marking per place, not per line.
- Nothing that breaks the per-line rings or the station fill.

**Non-Goals**

- Merging the rings. That is #23, and doing it here would break the fill.
- Labels. #25.
- Models of real buildings. These represent a mode.

## Decisions

### The rings stay, and the new marking is per place

The rings carry two things nothing else does: **which line**, and **whether a train is standing
there**. The second is a Milestone 4 feature and must not regress.

So the division is: **the model and the beacon say "a station is here"; the rings say which lines
and which is busy.** Four rings at Titiwangsa are correct — four lines really do stop there — while
four models would be a pile.

That also removes the dependency on #23: grouping stops by name is enough here, it is exact (no two
differently-named stops are within 150 m), and it is a drawing concern rather than a change to what
the app knows about the network.

### A place's position comes from its stops' drawn positions

Not from the feed's coordinates, which are up to 105 m out, and not from a fresh `pointAt` call,
which would miss the corridor offset and the camera-dependent gap.

The stations already compute drawn positions each time the camera changes. A place is the mean of
its members' drawn positions, computed in the same pass, so it moves with them.

### Two representations, and the crossfade is the hard part

`ScenegraphLayer` for the model, a column for the beacon. The crossfade is opacity driven by zoom,
and the thing to get right is that **the sum should not look like a gap**: two layers each at half
opacity read as one faint thing, not as a transition. Cross-fading over a narrow zoom band, with the
beacon reaching full opacity before the model has fully gone, avoids the dip.

Neither layer has a pixel-size floor, so both size from the camera using the relation already in
`src/map/trains.ts`.

### The beacon has to clear the buildings, and that is its whole job

A beacon shorter than the roofline solves nothing: being hidden by buildings is the problem it
exists for. It has to rise above them — and liberty's buildings are real heights, so "above the
roofline" is a real number in the city centre and a small one elsewhere.

A single height is the simple answer. A height that varies with the buildings around it would be
better and needs building data we do not have at hand; not now.

The risk in the other direction is a forest of columns. Translucency, a narrow width, and fading out
where stations are dense are the levers. This is taste and wants eyes.

### Colour

A beacon in the line's colour reads immediately. A place served by several lines has no single
colour, and blending them gives mud.

The workable answer: a place served by one line takes that line's colour; a place served by several
takes a neutral, which also distinguishes interchanges at a glance — useful information rather than
a compromise. Worth checking by eye, because "neutral" over a pale map and over a near-black one are
different colours, and the panels have just learned that lesson.

### Constraints carried from the train models

Expensive to rediscover, so stated plainly:

- **`getColor` replaces a material's base colour factor and multiplies a base-colour texture.** So
  per-instance tinting only works with shading baked into a texture. This cost one design iteration
  on the trains.
- **Model axes are +X forward, +Y left, +Z up, in metres** — not glTF's usual Y-up, because deck.gl
  applies no axis conversion. A Blender export needs "+Y Up" turned off.
- Models are generated by a committed script and replaceable by hand, like the trains.

### Proportions, by how often they are seen

The feed has four LRT lines, two MRT, one monorail, one BRT. **The LRT model is seen four times as
often as any other** and deserves the most care. The BRT stop is seen least and can be simplest.

Real proportions will not work literally, for the same reason the trains are short and fat: a real
platform is 150 m long and 10 m wide, which at the zoom where the model appears is a sliver. Some
exaggeration of width is necessary, and it should be deliberate rather than accidental.

## Risks / Trade-offs

**Another draw call per station, twice over.** 160 places with a model and a beacon, on top of 187
rings and up to 200 trains. deck.gl instances well and these are static between camera changes, but
it wants measuring rather than assuming.

**Clutter.** The failure mode is a map so full of furniture that the trains are lost in it, which
would defeat the point. The spec has a requirement about this precisely because it is the likely way
this goes wrong.

**Underground stations get a platform in the open air.** The feed has no height data, the trains
already carry the same assumption, and the app says so. Adding structures makes it more visible
without making it more wrong.

**The crossfade band is taste.** Too narrow and it flickers; too wide and both are faint at once.
Needs eyes.

## Migration Plan

None. Additive: the rings and the station fill are untouched.
